        Motion with a job: writes slide into the list and raise the memtable "pool" (wave on the surface). A full memtable seals into a dashed capsule, then drops into L0 as a fresh sand layer (green outline). When L0 piles up, those files press together (dashed amber, squeezing) and sink as one denser layer into L1 and below. The seismograph traces activity and spikes on flushes and compactions. Crash: the cover flips up, the button arms, then the whole instrument dims, the signal flatlines, and recovery steps print one by one before it comes back online.

        a database
        you can kill.

        NebulaDB stores your keys like rock stores time: in layers. Fresh writes settle on top, older data is pressed down and compacted. We killed it with kill -9 three thousand times. It never lost a write it said OK to.

        3,000kill -9 crashes in the crash test

        How a write travels

        From your command to rock, in five steps.

        01 · wal

        Appended to the write-ahead log with a checksum, then fsynced. Now it survives a crash.

        02 · memtable

        Inserted into a sorted skip list in memory. Reads see it instantly.

        03 · flush

        A full memtable is written out as an immutable, sorted SSTable in L0.

        04 · compaction

        Overlapping files are merged downward; old versions and deleted keys are dropped.

        05 · read

        Newest first. Bloom filters skip files that can't hold the key; hot blocks stay cached.
